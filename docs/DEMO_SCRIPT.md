# PDX Benefits Navigator — Demo Script

**Format:** 5 minutes total
**Structure:** 60s problem → 3min live demo → 60s vision/close
**Practice goal:** Tight enough to nail under 4:30, leaving buffer

---

## ⏱️ 0:00 – 0:45 — The Hook (Problem)

**Open standing, screen showing the landing page with the animated $1.2B reveal in the background.**

> *"Last year in Oregon, families left **one-point-two billion dollars** in unclaimed government benefits on the table."*

[Pause 2 seconds. Let it land.]

> *"Not because they didn't qualify. **Because they didn't know they qualified.**"*

> *"SNAP. The Oregon Health Plan. Childcare subsidies worth $12,000 a year. Portland's Renter Relocation Assistance — that's $4,500 right now in your pocket if your landlord just raised your rent more than 10%. Most Portlanders have **never heard of it.**"*

> *"The system requires you to navigate **40 different programs**, across **four levels of government**, in **only English** — when 12% of Portland speaks something else at home."*

[Beat]

> *"We built PDX Benefits Navigator to fix that — in one day."*

**Click `/demo/maria` tab.**

## ⏱️ 1:00 – 2:30 — The Live Demo (Money Shot)

**Click "Analyze" — loading state plays for ~3 seconds.**

> *"In three seconds, Opus 4.7 is running Maria's intake against twenty programs — federal, state, Multnomah County, City of Portland — and grading her against the **2026 Federal Poverty Level**, household composition, employment status, and event triggers like her recent rent increase."*

**Results page renders. The Big Number animates from $0 to $24,400.**

> *"**Twenty-four thousand four hundred dollars a year.**"*

[Pause 2 seconds — let the audience absorb the number.]

**Point to the federal vs. PDX comparison chart.**

> *"Federal tools found nine thousand two hundred. We found **two-and-a-half times more.** Look at where the difference comes from:"*

**Scroll to benefit cards, point to the starred hidden gems:**

> *"**Portland Renter Relocation Assistance** — $4,500. Because her landlord raised rent over 10%, the city legally owes her this. **She had no idea.**"*

> *"**ERDC childcare subsidy** — $12,000 a year. The single biggest line item, almost never surfaced by national tools."*

> *"**SUN Schools** — wraparound services at her kids' school. **PCEF home weatherization** — $5,000 in efficiency upgrades the Portland Clean Energy Fund will cover. **Portland Water Bureau Discount.** **Transportation Wallet.**"*

> *"Every single one is a real program with a real application URL. The packet she downloads has QR codes that take her directly to each one."*

**Click "Download Application Packet" — PDF previews briefly.**

> *"Pre-filled cover page. One application per program. Documents she needs to gather. **She walks out of this site with everything she needs.**"*

**Click the language toggle: EN → ES. Page re-renders in Spanish.**

> *"Maria's primary language is Spanish. Watch."*

[The room reacts.]

> *"Every program, every recommendation, every next step — translated in real time. **Same model, no separate translation pipeline.** Vietnamese, Russian, Mandarin work the same way."*

## Speaker Notes

### Pacing
- Total: ~4 minutes 45 seconds of content
- Aim to land at 4:30, leaving 30 seconds of buffer
- The Big Number animation gets its own pause — don't talk over it

### Energy curve
- 0:00–0:45: serious, problem-focused, controlled
- 0:45–2:30: building energy, leaning into the demo
- 2:30–3:30: confident, "look how much else this does"
- 3:30–4:15: factual, hitting the criteria
- 4:15–5:00: vision, emotional landing

### Key phrases to lock in (memorize verbatim)
- *"One-point-two billion dollars left on the table."*
- *"Not because they didn't qualify. **Because they didn't know they qualified.**"*
- *"Two-and-a-half times more than federal-only tools."*
- *"She walks out of this site with everything she needs."*
- *"PDX Benefits Navigator is a product, not a prototype."*

### What NOT to say
- Don't apologize for anything ("we didn't get to...", "we ran out of time...")
- Don't hedge with "kind of," "sort of," "basically"
- Don't read off the slide — look at the judges
- Don't talk about the stack unless asked ("Next.js, Opus 4.7, Anthropic API" is fine if asked, otherwise skip)

### Body language
- Stand still during the Big Number animation — let it be the focus
- Use open hands when describing dollar values
- Make eye contact with all three judges, not just the friendly one
- End with hands at sides, not in pockets

## Q&A Prep

**Likely questions and your answers:**

> *"How accurate is the eligibility matching?"*

*"Every match comes with a confidence level — high, medium, or low. For medium-confidence matches, we explicitly flag what needs verification. The legal disclaimer points users to Oregon Law Center for real cases. We're not replacing case workers — we're making their first 90 minutes of intake unnecessary."*

> *"What about data privacy?"*

*"Nothing is stored. Every analysis runs in a stateless API call. No database, no logs, no user accounts. You close the tab, the data is gone. That's a deliberate design choice — undocumented residents need to be able to use this without fear."*

> *"How would you partner with the city?"*

*"Three obvious paths. One: white-label this for 211info, who currently does this work over the phone. Two: embed it as a self-service tool on Portland's website, freeing up DCHS staff for complex cases. Three: package it as a caseworker tool for outreach orgs like JOIN and CCC. I'd start with conversations next week."*

> *"What's the moat? Couldn't anyone build this?"*

*"The moat isn't the tech. The moat is the **maintained, verified, hyperlocal program database** combined with the trust relationship with Portland-area service providers. That's what takes months to build. We started today."*

> *"How do you handle program changes over time?"*

*"Programs change quarterly at most. The scraping pipeline runs on a schedule, AI extracts updates, humans verify. For a real deployment, I'd add a community contribution system — caseworkers report changes they encounter."*

> *"Why Opus over Sonnet?"*

*"Eligibility reasoning has edge cases — a veteran who's also a senior who also has children, for example. Opus handles those compound scenarios more reliably. And the translation quality across Vietnamese and Russian is meaningfully better. Cost difference for our use case is trivial — about $0.45 per analysis."*

## Backup Plans

### If the wifi dies
- Switch to screencast video on tab 5
- Narrate over it identically
- Don't acknowledge the issue more than once: *"Going to roll the recording — same demo."*

### If the API rate-limits
- Pre-cache the three demo scenario responses in localStorage
- Hit the cached version, narrate as if it's live

- Acknowledge: "Great question, want to come back to that at the end — let me finish this thread first."
- Don't get derailed

- Click `/demo/maria` and start talking about the comparison chart
- The visuals will guide you back
